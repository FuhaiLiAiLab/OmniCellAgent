# Review

## Summary
This paper presents a comprehensive analysis of gene expression in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) using single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) data. The authors identify several key genes and pathways that are dysregulated in PDAC, including HCG17, MLXIP, RAB11FIP3, and others. They provide mechanistic hypotheses for how these genes contribute to the development and progression of PDAC, and suggest potential experimental validation strategies. The paper also discusses the clinical relevance of these findings and how they might be used to develop new therapeutic strategies for PDAC.

## Strengths
1. The paper provides a novel perspective on the molecular mechanisms underlying PDAC, identifying key genes and pathways that have not been extensively studied in previous work.
2. The authors propose several testable hypotheses that could lead to new insights into the biology of PDAC and potentially to the development of more effective therapeutic strategies.
3. The paper is well-structured and clearly written, with a logical flow from the introduction to the methods, results, and discussion.

## Weaknesses
1. The study is limited to a single dataset and does not include validation on additional datasets or experimental samples, which could limit the generalizability of the findings.
2. The authors do not provide any experimental validation of their hypotheses, instead relying on literature evidence and existing knowledge.
3. The paper could benefit from a more detailed discussion of the limitations of the study and potential directions for future research.

## Questions
1. How do the authors plan to validate their hypotheses experimentally? What specific experiments would be needed to confirm the proposed mechanisms?
2. Can the authors comment on the potential impact of stromal cell contamination on the interpretation of the scRNA-seq data? How might this affect the conclusions drawn in the paper?
3. The authors mention several existing clinical trials and therapeutic strategies related to the genes and pathways identified in the study. How might the findings in this paper influence the design or interpretation of these trials?
