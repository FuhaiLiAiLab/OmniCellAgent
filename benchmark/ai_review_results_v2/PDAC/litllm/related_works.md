## Related Works Section

Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) is characterized by a complex interplay of genetic and metabolic alterations that drive its aggressive phenotype. The Warburg effect, a hallmark of cancer metabolism, is particularly pronounced in PDAC, where tumor cells preferentially utilize glycolysis even in the presence of oxygen. This metabolic reprogramming is often regulated by hypoxia-inducible factors (HIFs), particularly HIF1A, which orchestrate the cellular response to hypoxic conditions prevalent in the tumor microenvironment. Recent studies have highlighted the role of non-coding RNAs, such as lncRNAs, in modulating these metabolic pathways, although their specific contributions in PDAC remain underexplored.
