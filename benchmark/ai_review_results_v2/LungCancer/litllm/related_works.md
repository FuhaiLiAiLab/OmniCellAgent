## Related Works Section

Lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) is a major subtype of non-small cell lung cancer characterized by complex genetic and epigenetic alterations. Recent advances in single-cell RNA sequencing have enabled the dissection of tumor heterogeneity at an unprecedented resolution, revealing insights into the cellular and molecular mechanisms driving tumor progression and resistance. The Warburg effect, a hallmark of cancer metabolism, underscores the metabolic reprogramming observed in LUAD, often linked to mitochondrial dysfunction and oxidative stress (Vander Heiden et al., 2009). The role of epigenetic regulation, particularly through chromatin remodeling complexes like ATAC, is increasingly recognized in modulating gene expression and immune evasion in cancer (Corces et al., 2018). Furthermore, the emerging concept of cuproptosis highlights the intricate balance of metal ion homeostasis in cancer cell survival and death (Tsvetkov et al., 2022). This report builds on these foundational studies by integrating single-cell transcriptomics with pathway enrichment and mechanistic hypotheses, offering novel insights into the early-stage dynamics of LUAD.
