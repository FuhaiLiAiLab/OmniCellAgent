### Review Summary

#### Objectives and Main Findings
The report aims to identify key dysfunctional genes and pathways in Pancreatic Ductal Adenocarcinoma (PDAC) using a multi-omics approach. The main findings include the identification of differentially expressed genes (DEGs) such as HCG17, LRRC37A3, and RAB11FIP3, with significant upregulation, and COX7C, UQCRB, and NDUFA4, with significant downregulation. The report suggests that these DEGs are involved in pathways related to mitochondrial dysfunction, vesicle trafficking, and stress response. Three mechanistic hypotheses are proposed, focusing on metabolic zonation, vesicular hijacking, and synthetic lethality.

#### Statistical and Methodological Rigour
The report lacks transparency in its statistical and methodological approaches. Specific details on normalization, batch correction, and statistical tests used for differential expression analysis are missing, which undermines the robustness of the findings. The reported p-values are extremely low, suggesting potential statistical artifacts or overpowered tests. Additionally, the analysis does not adequately address cell-type confounding, which is a critical flaw given the heterogeneous nature of the samples.

#### Mechanistic Plausibility
The hypotheses presented are mechanistically plausible but require further validation. The HCG17-driven metabolic zonation hypothesis is intriguing but hinges on the validity of the DEG analysis, which is questionable due to potential confounding. The vesicular hijacking hypothesis is supported by literature but may face challenges in targeting due to systemic toxicity concerns. The synthetic lethality hypothesis is novel but lacks direct evidence linking FBXO42 to PDAC metabolism.

#### Proposed Validation Experiments
The proposed validation experiments are well-conceived but need refinement. The experiments for HCG17 involve spatial transcriptomics and CRISPR-Cas13d knockdown, which are appropriate but should be preceded by a re-analysis of the DEG data. The vesicular hijacking experiments should include more specific controls to account for potential confounders. The synthetic lethality experiments would benefit from additional in vivo validation to confirm the findings.

#### Reference Verification
Several citations appear to be fabricated or future-dated, including those by Ramsey et al. (2025), Akrami et al. (2025), and Zhou et al. (2025). These references undermine the credibility of the report and should be removed or replaced with verified sources.

#### Suggestions for Improvement
1. **Methodological Transparency:** Provide detailed information on the statistical methods, normalization, and batch correction used in the analysis.
2. **Re-analysis of Data:** Conduct a cell-type-aware differential expression analysis to address confounding issues.
3. **Reference Verification:** Remove fabricated citations and replace them with verified literature.
4. **Validation Experiments:** Refine the experimental design to include appropriate controls and consider additional in vivo studies.
5. **Literature Contextualization:** Include foundational literature on key concepts like the Warburg effect and HIF1A biology to better contextualize the hypotheses.

OVERALL_SCORE: 5
