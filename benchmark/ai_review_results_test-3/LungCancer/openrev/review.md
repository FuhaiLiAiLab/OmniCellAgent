# Review

## Summary
This paper presents a comprehensive analysis of differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in lung cancer using single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) data. The authors identify four key genes—ZZZ3, TAFAZZIN, SEPTIN2, and WASHC2A—that are significantly dysregulated in lung cancer. Through detailed functional analysis, they reveal that ZZZ3 drives epigenetic cloaking and immune evasion, TAFAZZIN mediates metabolic reprogramming, SEPTIN2 facilitates cytoskeletal dynamics and invasion, and WASHC2A regulates endosomal trafficking and cellular plasticity. The study also explores potential drug targets based on these genes and pathways, suggesting that combining HDAC inhibitors with immunotherapy could enhance treatment outcomes. Overall, this work provides valuable insights into the molecular mechanisms of lung cancer and proposes promising therapeutic strategies for this disease.

## Strengths
1. The paper provides a comprehensive analysis of differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in lung cancer using single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) data. This approach allows for a detailed examination of gene expression patterns and identifies key genes that are significantly dysregulated in lung cancer.
2. The authors identify four key genes—ZZZ3, TAFAZZIN, SEPTIN2, and WASHC2A—that are significantly dysregulated in lung cancer. They provide detailed functional analysis of these genes, revealing their roles in various cellular processes and their potential as therapeutic targets.
3. The study explores potential drug targets based on the identified genes and pathways. The authors suggest that combining HDAC inhibitors with immunotherapy could enhance treatment outcomes for lung cancer, providing promising therapeutic strategies for this disease.

## Weaknesses
1. The study primarily focuses on analyzing existing scRNA-seq data and may not introduce new experimental models or datasets. This could limit the novelty and generalizability of the findings.
2. While the paper identifies key genes and pathways involved in lung cancer, it may not provide sufficient experimental evidence to support the proposed therapeutic strategies. Additional experiments are needed to validate the effectiveness of these strategies and ensure they are not just theoretical possibilities.
3. The study may not adequately address the potential limitations and challenges of the proposed therapeutic strategies. For example, the combination of HDAC inhibitors with immunotherapy could have adverse effects or interact with other medications, which should be carefully considered and discussed.

## Questions
1. How do the authors plan to validate the proposed therapeutic strategies, such as combining HDAC inhibitors with immunotherapy? Are there any ongoing or planned experiments to demonstrate the effectiveness of these approaches in lung cancer treatment?
2. What are the potential limitations and challenges of the proposed therapeutic strategies? How can these be addressed to ensure safe and effective treatment outcomes for patients with lung cancer?
3. Are there any plans to conduct new experiments or analyze additional datasets to further investigate the identified genes and pathways in lung cancer? This could help to confirm the findings and provide more comprehensive insights into the molecular mechanisms of this disease.
