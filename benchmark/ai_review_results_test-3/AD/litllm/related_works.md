## Related Works Section

Alzheimer's Disease (AD) is a complex neurodegenerative disorder characterized by progressive cognitive decline. The amyloid cascade hypothesis, proposed by Hardy and Selkoe (2002), suggests that amyloid-beta accumulation is a primary event in AD pathogenesis. However, recent studies, such as De Strooper and Karran (2016), emphasize the importance of cellular and molecular mechanisms, including mitochondrial dysfunction and neuroinflammation, in AD progression.

The current report by OmniCellAgent aligns with these perspectives by identifying key dysfunctional genes and pathways in AD through single-cell transcriptomics. The identification of DEGs such as NDUFA10 and MAP3K15 supports the notion of mitochondrial dysfunction and neuroinflammation as critical components of AD pathology.
