# Review

## Summary
This paper presents a comprehensive analysis of single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) data from the OmniCellTOSG dataset to identify key genes and pathways involved in Alzheimer's Disease (AD). The authors identify several differentially expressed genes (DEGs) and pathways, including upregulation of long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) and stress kinases, and downregulation of metabolic and translational machinery. They propose three mechanistic hypotheses to explain these findings, which are supported by literature validation and pathway enrichment analysis. The authors also suggest potential therapeutic targets based on their findings.

## Strengths
1. The paper provides a novel and comprehensive analysis of scRNA-seq data in AD, identifying several key genes and pathways that have not been extensively studied in the context of AD.
2. The authors propose three mechanistic hypotheses to explain their findings, which are supported by literature validation and pathway enrichment analysis.
3. The paper suggests potential therapeutic targets based on their findings, which could have implications for the development of new treatments for AD.

## Weaknesses
1. The authors should provide more detailed information about the scRNA-seq data, such as the number of samples and the quality control measures used.
2. The authors should discuss the potential limitations and biases of their analysis, such as the possibility that some of the identified genes or pathways may be confounders or mediators rather than direct causes of AD.
3. The authors should provide more detailed information about the proposed therapeutic targets, such as potential side effects or interactions with other medications.

## Questions
1. How do the authors control for the potential confounding variables in their analysis, such as age, gender, or other demographic factors?
2. How do the authors ensure that their identified genes and pathways are not just markers or mediators of AD, but actual causal factors?
3. What are the potential ethical considerations or privacy concerns associated with the proposed therapeutic targets, especially if they involve genetic testing or other sensitive information?
